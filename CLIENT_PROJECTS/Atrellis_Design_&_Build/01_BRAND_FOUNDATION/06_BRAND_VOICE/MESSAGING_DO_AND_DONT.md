# Messaging Do and Don't

## Do

- Write in a clear and premium way
- Use short, direct sentences when possible
- Explain broad services with control and hierarchy
- Make next steps feel easy to understand
- Use confidence-building wording
- Use grounded, believable claims
- Let structure carry part of the persuasion
- Write like a serious modern brand

## Don't

- Overstuff the copy with adjectives
- Use vague "quality" claims with no meaning
- Sound like a cheap lead-gen page
- Sound like a generic contractor brochure
- Sound like a startup SaaS company with no real-world grounding
- Use inflated future-feature language as if it already exists
- Write walls of text when structure would help more

## Good direction examples

Better:
- A more organized way to move forward with renovation-related solutions
- Clearer guidance, stronger presentation, and a more confident customer experience
- Designed to make evaluation and next steps easier

Worse:
- We are the leading premier trusted one-stop provider for all your needs
- Innovative world-class excellence for your dream transformation
- Revolutionizing the industry with cutting-edge solutions

## CTA tone rule

CTA language should feel:
- helpful
- clear
- intentional
- easy to act on

It should not feel:
- aggressive
- gimmicky
- overhyped
- confusing

## Brand message test

Before approving important copy, ask:
- Does this sound clear?
- Does this sound trustworthy?
- Does this sound premium?
- Does this sound structured?
- Does this sound believable?
