# Category Framing

## Why category framing matters

Atrellis spans a broad set of services and product-system areas.

Without the right category framing, the brand could easily become:
- too broad,
- too vague,
- too contractor-like,
- or too tech-branded without enough real-world grounding.

This file defines how to frame the category correctly.

## Primary framing

The clearest primary framing is:

A premium design-and-build and product-systems brand with a modern digital customer experience.

This framing is useful because it captures:
- service capability,
- systems thinking,
- premium positioning,
- and digital differentiation.

## Secondary framing options

Use these when context requires different emphasis:

### Option 1
Premium renovation and systems experience brand

Use when the audience is more consumer-facing and less interested in technical distinctions.

### Option 2
Design-and-build and architectural systems specialist

Use when the audience needs a more capability-focused framing.

### Option 3
Digitally enabled design-and-build platform foundation

Use internally when discussing the long-term strategic direction, not as the main public tagline.

## Category warning

Do not frame Atrellis as:
- just a contractor
- just an interior brand
- just a roofing company
- just a blinds company
- just a tech platform

Each of those is too narrow for the intended long-term structure.

## Framing rule

The category framing should always preserve both truths:

1. Atrellis operates in real service and product-system categories.
2. Atrellis is building a more modern and more structured customer experience than those categories usually provide.

## Public-facing rule

Public-facing copy should sound clear and grounded.

Internal strategy language can be broader and more future-facing.

Do not confuse those two modes.
